Selective Sweep Simulator

A new beneficial mutation appears on one chromosome. As it spreads, it drags along the DNA around it and wipes out nearby genetic variation. That is a selective sweep.

How much better the mutation is: carriers leave (1 + s) times as many offspring.
Expected crossovers per chromosome per generation across the whole region (Morgans).
Press Run to release one beneficial mutation.

What to try

  1. Double s. What happens to the time to fixation and the sweep width?
  2. Set R = 0, then set R high. How does the valley change?
  3. Run the same settings 5 times. How variable are the results?

The model

A population of 400 chromosomes reproduces each generation; parents are picked at random, but carriers of the beneficial allele are (1 + s) times more likely to be picked. Each new chromosome is stitched together from two parents, switching parents at a random number of crossover points (average R) placed anywhere along the region. The region holds 200 common neutral variants that start out evenly diverse, with the beneficial mutation in the middle. Diversity at a site is the chance that two random chromosomes carry different versions of it.

Bottleneck Simulator

Two populations start out identical. The wild one stays large. The crop one is founded by a few plants, stays small for a while, then grows back. What does the bottleneck do to diversity, and to harmful mutations?

Plants that start the crop population.
Generations the crop stays at F plants before recovering.
Fitness cost of carrying two copies.
New harmful mutations per gamete per generation.
Large size N = 500 plants. Run lasts 200 generations.

What to try

  1. Run a sweep and a bottleneck. How do the two diversity plots differ in shape?
  2. 2 founders vs 50 founders.
  3. A short bottleneck (2 generations) vs a long one (40).
  4. Weak (0.001) vs strong (0.1) harmful mutations. Weak ones drift up in a small population; strong ones get purged anyway.

The point: in a small population, chance outweighs weak selection, so mildly harmful mutations can rise and even fix.

The model

Each generation, every plant is replaced by offspring of two parents; healthier parents are more likely to be picked. Each parent passes on one of its two copies of every harmful site, at random (they are unlinked). Every new gamete also picks up new harmful mutations (on average U), each at a new site; a plant with one copy loses h·sd of its fitness, with two copies sd, and costs multiply across sites. Before generation 0 the large population runs 200 generations so it already carries a realistic set of rare harmful mutations. The crop is then founded by F plants drawn from it.

Diversity is the chance that two random copies of a neutral site differ. The chromosome carries 200 common neutral variants spread evenly along it (about one crossover per chromosome per generation), set up the same way as on the sweep tab. Even the “large” population drifts a little: 500 plants is small next to a real wild species.
