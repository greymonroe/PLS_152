Capstone: Final Group Presentation

The final for this class is a group presentation (5–10 minutes) exploring a plant genetics topic of your group’s choice, presented in class on December 1 and 3. Groups are 4–5 people. It’s worth 30% of your grade (100 points, plus up to 5 extra credit).

Start thinking about it now

The capstone comes at the end, but the best presentations start much earlier — in two ways.

The topic. Ideas will come at you all quarter: a lecture that hooks you, a reading, the cacao tour, a guest speaker, something you dig up researching your midterm. When something makes you think “I want to know more about that,” write it down — that list is where good capstone topics come from. Like the midterm, focused beats broad, and the same three doors apply: a problem plant genetics can help solve, an open question, or how a genetic technology works.

The group. This is a group project, which makes the class itself part of the assignment: sit next to someone new, talk during the break, compare answers during in-class activities. The people you meet in the first weeks may well become your group — and groups that form around a shared interest tend to have more fun with this. If you’d rather be assigned to a group, that’s completely fine too; the preference survey (due Thu Oct 29) asks which you want.

What you submit

  • Presentation file (PDF or PPTX) — one upload per group on Canvas; anyone in the group can submit and it counts for everyone. Due Dec 1 at 3:10 PM regardless of which day your group presents.
  • Individual contributions statement — every student submits their own short, confidential statement: what you contributed (slides, research, figures, organizing, presenting sections…) and how the work was divided. A few sentences is plenty. Only the teaching team sees it; it’s how we make sure credit lands where the work happened.

How it’s graded

The full rubric is attached to the Canvas assignment. The group grade covers depth of content, accuracy, use of references (at least 5 citations on your slides), introduction / body / conclusion structure, clarity, slide design, collaboration, and time management (stay in the 5–10 minute window). Two pieces are graded per student: your individual contribution (from your statement) and audience engagement — being present and engaged during the other groups’ presentations, including asking at least one thoughtful question across the two days. Up to 5 extra credit points for genuinely creative insights or framings.
